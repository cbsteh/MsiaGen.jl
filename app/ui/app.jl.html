MsiaGen generates daily weather (minimum and maximum air temperature, wind speed and rainfall) from monthly statistics, kept for each site in its folder in the data folder:

  • site-obs.csv: observed daily weather (optional)
  • site-stats.csv: the monthly statistics of every year, which MsiaGen generates from
  • site-sim.csv: the generated daily weather

Open a site to design its statistics (steps 1 to 5), generate its weather, and check how well the generated weather matches the statistics. A design starts from the site's observed weather if it has any, otherwise from typical Malaysian values. New site designs a site from scratch.

Each part of the weather (temperature, wind and rainfall) is designed in three layers:

  1. Start year: the whole year, a seasonal curve plus values that apply to all months.
  2. Each month: change any month away from the whole-year values.
  3. End year: how much the whole-year values change by the end year, and the path they take. The years between follow that path, in all months.

To modify an existing weather record instead of starting from typical values, start from a daily weather file: every control then starts from that file's average year.

MsiaGen needs four statistics per month for each of minimum (tmin) and maximum (tmax) air temperature: mean, sd, rlag and skew. Switch between tmin and tmax above the controls; the settings of both are kept.

Typical start values: tmin 22 °C, tmax 33 °C, amplitude 1 °C, warmest day 183; sd 0.7 and 1.25 °C, rlag 0.25 and 0.2, skew −0.1 and −0.8.

MsiaGen needs three statistics per month for wind: mean, sd and rlag. There is no skew: the daily departures come from a Weibull distribution whose shape follows from sd relative to mean (a larger sd / mean gives more gusty spikes).

MsiaGen never lets wind fall below 0.1 m/s; with a low mean and a high sd, many days sit on that floor and the real average comes out higher than set. Typical start values: 1.6 m/s, amplitude 0.2, windiest day 45, sd 0.3, rlag 0.2.

MsiaGen needs three statistics per month for rain: the total, pww and pwd. Higher pww gives longer wet spells; lower pwd gives longer dry spells. Together they set how many days are wet: days × pwd / (1 − pww + pwd), rounded (at least 1 in a month with rain).

Seasonality is how much wetter the wettest time of year is than average, and drier the driest. Typical start values: 2460 mm, 30%, driest day 183, pww 0.58, pwd 0.42 (15 rain days in a 30-day month).

The points and rain days in the chart are illustrative: drawn with MsiaGen's own equations so you can see what the numbers mean. They are not the generated weather.

Generate weather {{ site }}

Simulated against specified statistics
Click for more information

MsiaGen generates rain first (a two-state Markov chain for wet and dry days, Gamma-distributed amounts), then temperature (skew-normal, lag-1 autoregression) and wind (Weibull, lag-1). Each month is generated until its statistics fall within the fit tolerance; for a month that never does, the best attempt is kept and listed in the messages.

The result is written to {{ statsName().replace('-stats.csv', '-sim.csv') }} in the site's folder.

Generate weather {{ site }}

Simulated against specified statistics
Click for more information

The simulated weather is measured with the same method that builds a stats file, and every monthly statistic of every year is compared with its specified value in the stats file (error = simulated − specified). Outside tol counts the values further from the specified value than the generator's fit tolerance.

The Fit mark grades the share of values outside the tolerance: good under 10%, fair under 20%, acceptable up to 30%, poor over 30%.

In the charts, each dot is one month of one year; the dashed line is 1:1 and the shaded band is the fit tolerance.

About MsiaGen

A stochastic daily weather generator for Malaysia's tropical climate: skew-normal air temperatures, Weibull wind speed and Gamma rainfall, with first-order autoregression and a two-state Markov chain for wet and dry days.
